The roadmap creates seven decisions that keep coming back: at every stage gate, on every rollout wave and with every exception. This page names one Accountable owner for each of them before the first conflict arises.

Initiative IDs and names (I1-I8) and the quarters Q1-Q12 match the roadmap exactly. Q1-Q12 are proposed relative planning quarters, not Citi’s historical dates or regulatory deadlines. Decision IDs G1-G7 are used here and on the dashboard. They are separate from the roadmap's dependency IDs D1-D10.

The sources confirm the role titles and the Board's oversight committee. The R, A, C and I assignments, the rationales and the enforcement mechanisms describe our proposed governance model, built from those sources and reviewed with the Roadmap Sequencer. They are not documented Citi policy. Citi does not publish its internal management committee charters or decision rights at this level of detail.

Accountable rationale and proposed enforcement

For each row: why this role holds the decision in our proposed model, and the proposed mechanism that keeps the assignment real when nobody is looking. The second question is the same one our repository's branch protection answers for a merge.

G1 Budget allocation | Accountable: CFO

Decision: Release, hold or reallocate the next funding tranche across I1-I8 when a stage gate is reached.

Why the CFO: In our proposed governance model, the CFO is Accountable for releasing or reallocating approved transformation funding, subject to applicable delegated authority, because the CFO owns Citi's firmwide expense and investment plan. Making the CFO Accountable, with the COO's program office Responsible for presenting the gate evidence, separates the executive who certifies progress from the one who releases funds.

Proposed enforcement: Funding is released in tranches tied to gate IDs (Q4 for I4-I6, Q7 for I7-I8). No spending commitment can be raised against an unreleased tranche.

G2 Initiative prioritization | Accountable: COO

Decision: When I4-I8 compete for the same data engineers or platform capacity, decide which initiative or wave goes first.

Why the COO: Citi's COO heads the overall Transformation, so he is the one executive who sees both paths (reporting: I4 and I6 to I7; lending: I5 to I8) and the regulatory commitments behind them. Choosing between a regulatory reporting wave and a loan migration wave trades remediation risk against business benefit, which is a firmwide call, not a technology one.

Proposed enforcement: The quarterly delivery backlog is frozen at each gate review. Adding work mid-quarter requires a logged change request signed by the COO.

G3 Architecture standards | Accountable: Head of T&BE

Decision: Approve or refuse a deviation from the shared data platform (I2) or the strategic loan processing platform (I8), such as a local interface, a manual feed or a product-specific build.

Why the Head of T&BE: This role runs Citi's technology function and bears the long-run cost when a workaround creates another feed outside lineage. Business heads are Consulted because I8 deviations often come from real client needs. They cannot approve them, or the shared platform would fragment one exception at a time.

Proposed enforcement: Release pipelines for I4, I6 and I8 reject deployments that use an unregistered interface. Every approved deviation carries an expiry date.

G4 Data governance | Accountable: Head of EDO

Decision: Set the quality rules, critical data elements and access policy that every business must meet, and decide disputes over data ownership.

Why the Head of EDO: In 2024 Citi created this role to lead the enterprise data office and placed the Chief Data Office under it (B2), which makes it the natural home for data-policy authority in our model. The Head of Banking and the Controller own their data and do the remediation (Responsible). If each could set its own threshold, I2's 99% check rate would mean different things in different businesses.

Design choice: in 2024 Citi named its COO and Head of T&BE as co-leads of the data program (B2). We keep that co-leadership for the program but give the policy decision to a single role, because two Accountables on one decision is the failure the RACI exists to prevent.

Proposed enforcement: The I2 platform blocks a feed from production use until every critical data element in it has a named owner and an approved rule in the I1 rulebook.

G5 Vendor and partnership decisions | Accountable: Head of T&BE

Decision: Approve, renew or exit the key technology vendors behind I2, I4, I5, I6 and the I8 migration.

Why the Head of T&BE: Vendor choices for these initiatives lock in the architecture governed under G3, so the same executive must own both or procurement becomes a way around the architecture standards. The CRO is Consulted on third-party risk, which regulators examine closely. The CFO is Consulted on cost but does not choose the platform.

Gap: the sources do not name Citi's vendors for these platforms, so this row governs the decision type rather than specific contracts.

Proposed enforcement: No key contract can be signed without a G3 architecture-compliance sign-off and a completed third-party risk assessment on file.

G6 Authorize cutover or pause a rollout wave case specific | Accountable: COO

Decision: At each acceptance gate, decide whether a deployment goes live, continues in parallel or is paused.

Why the COO: The roadmap requires that a failed gate pause only its own path, and that success on one path does not authorize the other. One owner who sees both paths can stop an I7 wave and judge I8 on its own evidence: I5 acceptance and no unresolved shared I1/I2 defect affecting lending. The path owners (the Controller for I4, I6 and I7; the Head of Banking for I8) are Responsible for presenting gate evidence but cannot certify their own work. In our model the COO reports execution to the Board's Transformation Oversight Committee, so the cost of a wrong go-live falls on him.

Proposed enforcement: Legacy reporting spreadsheets and old loan processing stay switched on until a signed gate record exists. Retirement steps require that record's ID.

G7 Approve product exceptions to standard loan processing case specific | Accountable: CRO

Decision: Allow or refuse a loan product, client or deal to bypass the standard workflow designed in I3 and migrated in I8.

Why the CRO: Citi describes I3 as a wholesale credit risk target operating model, so the standard workflow is a risk control and each exception weakens it. The CRO is the independent function able to refuse a client-driven exception. The Head of Banking raises and justifies requests (Responsible) but would otherwise be approving their own exceptions.

Proposed enforcement: Every exception is logged with an expiry date and counted toward I8's acceptance gate ("no higher control-exception rate"), so exception creep shows up on the dashboard.
