2. Target audience

This site is designed for a transformation portfolio lead at a large multinational bank who must recommend whether to release the next wave of funding for regulatory reporting and North American wholesale loan modernization when data quality, operating readiness, and delivery capacity compete for attention.

3. Benefits to that audience

  • Transformation Roadmap: identify which deployment or expansion to defer when a data foundation or pilot acceptance gate fails, and trace the downstream consequences.
  • Governance RACI: identify the accountable decision-maker for recurring funding, architecture, data, vendor, prioritization, and cutover choices, along with who must contribute before a decision is made.
  • QBR Dashboard: use the quarterly review to connect initiative performance and organizational readiness to the governance decisions leadership must resolve.

4. Novelty statement

We organize this case around two operating paths: trusted regulatory reporting and North American wholesale loan processing. On the roadmap, I4 and I6 must both clear acceptance before I7 scales reporting, while I5 must prove a bounded workflow before I8 migrates the scoped loan portfolio. This makes the consequence of a failed gate visible: the affected rollout pauses rather than allowing progress elsewhere to substitute for its evidence. Carlos’s AML governance experience and Marggie’s Operations and Technology background inform our emphasis on ownership, control handoffs, and exception escalation. The source log separates Citi’s disclosed progress from our proposed quarters, pilot scopes, and acceptance thresholds. We also distinguish initial deployment from later expansion so that a successful pilot cannot count as enterprise completion. These choices give the reader a concrete sequencing argument to challenge and a consistent set of eight work packages against which to assess governance and performance measures.
